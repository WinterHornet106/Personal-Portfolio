01 — Job Growth Outlook

More AI Doesn't
Mean More Jobs.

And here's the irony that emerges from this data: Industries that use the most AI are actually those where there is job loss. Manufacturing, which is one of the highest industries in adopting AI, is projected to have the largest fall with almost 50% of its jobs at risk. The finance and education sectors, which use lesser AI than manufacturing, have more job opportunities.

Job Growth Projection by Industry — % of roles

This graph analyzes the projected job growth, stability, and decline in various sectors. The sectors that will experience high job growth include Finance and Education, while sectors that will experience a high rate of decline include Retail and Energy.

Key Insight Manufacturing has been a leader in adopting AI technology but still projects a 46.6% decline in jobs – worse than any other industry. Financial services have experienced 43.4% growth, while education saw a 42.1% increase in employment opportunities due to AI.

02 — Automation Risk

Nobody Is Completely Safe.
Not Even AI Experts.

The data challenges conventional wisdom: technical roles like Cybersecurity and Product Management show higher exposure to automation than many operational roles. This suggests that the most protected positions are those defined by layered human judgment rather than technical execution alone

Automation Risk Level by Job Title — % of roles

The distribution of risk across these ten roles suggests a new hierarchy of automation. Positions centered on high-level strategy and organizational culture show the highest "low risk" percentages, whereas roles focused on technical systems and project coordination face a more immediate transition toward AI integration.

Key Insight Cybersecurity Analysts face 41.8% high-risk exposure — more than Sales Managers or Marketing Specialists. Operations Managers show the lowest high-risk rate at just 22.7%. Operational complexity may be the job protection AI can't easily replicate.
AI is no longer a technical niche; it is the new universal language of the global workforce

03 — In-Demand Skills

The AI Era Rewards Both
Coders and Coordinators.

If you expected coding to dominate, you're half right. Python ties exactly with Project Management at the top of the skills chart. The data tells us the AI-augmented workforce isn't purely technical — it's hybrid. But not all skills offer equal protection. A closer look at automation risk by skill reveals that leading with Sales is the most exposed position you can be in.

Most In-Demand Skills — job postings count

This graph shows that while Python is highly in demand, Project Management is just as important. The presence of skills like UX/UI Design, Data Analysis, and Communication highlights that the AI job market values a balance of technical and non-technical abilities. The top 10 most in-demand skills across all industries show a blend of technical and interpersonal competencies. Project Management and Python lead with 60 job postings each, followed closely by Cybersecurity. Communication, while essential, ranks lowest in demand, suggesting that technical fluency is becoming a baseline expectation in the AI era.

Key Insight Success in the AI job market depends on a combination of technical skills and strong human-centered abilities like communication and project management.

The Takeaway

What This Means for You

The AI job market isn't the zero-sum replacement story the headlines suggest. It's a more nuanced reshuffling — and three findings should inform every worker's strategy going forward.

🏥

AI is everywhere, not just tech

Healthcare, Manufacturing, and Education are the real frontiers of AI adoption. If you're in a "non-tech" field, AI has already arrived in your workplace.

🔀

Hybrid skills are the moat

Project Management and Python tied at the top. The workers who thrive combine technical fluency with human coordination — neither alone is enough.

📈

Judgment, not code, drives growth

Finance and Education lead job growth because they require nuanced human judgment AI can augment but not replace. Operational complexity is your career shield.
