Overview

The project

Redesigning community for MEC.

A 12-week SFU IAT collaboration to reimagine the community and events experience for Mountain Equipment Company — connecting outdoor enthusiasts with local activities that match their gear, skill level, and interests.

My role spanned the full design process: research synthesis, concept exploration, high-fidelity prototyping, and presenting findings to MEC's product and marketing team.

Problem

The challenge

A loyal community with no digital home.

Mountain Equipment Company has an incredibly loyal customer base of outdoor enthusiasts — but their digital experience failed to reflect that community energy. Events were hard to discover, registration was clunky, and there was no way to connect with other members who shared your interests.

As part of a 12-week SFU IAT collaboration, our team was challenged to redesign the MEC app's community and events layer — from discovery all the way to post-event engagement.

Contribution & Insights

Research

Understanding the outdoor community.

We started with contextual interviews across 14 MEC members ranging from casual hikers to serious climbers. The consistent thread: people wanted to do more with MEC, but the app made them feel like a customer, not a community member.

8 Interviews Employee Discussions In Store Survey Journey Mapping

Key insight: the biggest friction wasn't awareness — it was trust. Users didn't know who was organizing events, what skill level was expected, or whether their gear was appropriate. Reducing uncertainty became our north star.

Design Process

Design Process

From insight to interface.

We explored three distinct directions in lo-fi: a social-first feed model, a map-based discovery model, and a curated editorial model. Through two rounds of testing we landed on a hybrid — map-based discovery with editorial curation for trust-building.

The final design introduced contextual event cards with gear recommendations, organizer profiles, and a lightweight social layer that let attendees connect before showing up.

Solution

Solution

Event discovery that builds confidence.

A smart events feed that surfaces relevant activities based on your gear, skill level, and location — no more scrolling through irrelevant listings.

Impact

Presented to MEC stakeholders.

The final prototype was presented to MEC's product and marketing team. The event card concept and gear-matching feature were flagged as immediate opportunities for their 2025 roadmap.

Reflection

Reflection

What I'd do differently.

I'd spend more time in the research phase validating our assumption that gear anxiety was the primary barrier — our sample skewed toward experienced users, and beginners likely had a very different set of fears. More divergent testing in lo-fi would have caught this earlier.

That said, shipping a prototype that sparked a real product conversation at a company like MEC was a genuine reminder that good design doesn't just look right — it has to land in the right room at the right time.
