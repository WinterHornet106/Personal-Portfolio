Overview

The project

Reimagining the identity for DDA.

A focused rebrand and microsite to showcase the finalists and winners of the Dutch Design Awards. The work centered on creating a flexible identity system that adapts across print, web, and exhibition signage.

Deliverables included a brand system, microsite templates, and guidelines for exhibition materials.

Problem

The challenge

An identity that couldn't keep up.

The Dutch Design Awards had an existing visual language that felt inconsistent across touchpoints — the microsite, exhibition materials, and print collateral all told slightly different stories. The challenge was to unify the brand without losing the craft-forward character the awards were known for.

The new system had to be flexible enough for rapid content updates during awards season, while still feeling considered and intentional at every scale.

Contribution & Insights

Competitive analysis

How leading design awards present themselves.

Studied Cannes Lions, D&AD, and Red Dot to understand how top-tier award programs balance prestige with accessibility. Key finding: the best programs lead with the work, not the brand.

Key insight

Modularity over rigidity.

The existing identity was too rigid for the variety of content — from video installations to graphic design to industrial objects. The new system needed to flex while keeping recognizable structure.

Design Process

Approach

Identity that adapts.

We prioritized legibility and modular presentation. The visual system supports both editorial pages and dynamic microsite templates for award entries.

Brand System Microsite Exhibition

This project balanced visual craft with pragmatic template design for rapid content updates.

Solution

Brand system

A flexible visual language.

A modular grid system with defined type hierarchy, a refined colour palette, and an icon set that scales from large-format exhibition panels down to mobile-first microsite cards.

Microsite

Templates built for speed.

Designed a component library that allowed the DDA editorial team to publish finalist and winner profiles without design involvement — cutting production time significantly during peak award season.

Reflection

Looking back

What I'd do differently.

I'd push for more stakeholder alignment earlier in the process. The curators and exhibition designers had strong opinions that surfaced late — earlier collaborative workshops would have avoided a few rounds of revision on the exhibition signage system.

The microsite template system worked well, but I underestimated how varied the content would be in practice. Building in more content-type flexibility from the start would have saved time downstream.
